- TF-IDF (term frequency × inverse document frequency) is a numerical statistic that reflects how important a term is to a document within a corpus [^1]
	- It is the <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">classical scoring function for the vector space model</mark> of [[202605011609 - information retrieval|information retrieval]]
	- A term is "important" for a document when it appears often *in that document* but rarely *in the corpus as a whole*
- Two ingredients
	- Term frequency $\text{tf}(t, d)$
		- How often term $t$ appears in the document $d$
		- Captures local importance — words that recur in a doc are likely on-topic
	- Inverse document frequency $\text{idf}(t)$
		- How rare a term $t$ is across the whole corpus
		- Captures global discriminativeness — words that appear in *every* doc (e.g. "the") tell you nothing about which doc to rank first

> [!MATH] TF-IDF
>
> $$\text{tfidf}(t, d) = \text{tf}(t, d) \cdot \text{idf}(t) = \text{tf}(t, d) \cdot \log\frac{N}{\text{df}(t)}$$
>
> where $N$ is the total number of documents and $\text{df}(t)$ is the number of documents containing $t$

- Common variants
	- Raw count tf vs. log-normalized tf — $1 + \log(\text{tf})$ dampens the effect of very high counts
	- Smoothed idf — $\log\!\left(\frac{N + 1}{\text{df}(t) + 1}\right) + 1$ to avoid divide-by-zero and over-weighting rare terms
	- L2-normalized vectors — divide each doc vector by its Euclidean norm so document length does not dominate
- Ranking with TF-IDF
	- Each document becomes a sparse vector indexed by vocabulary terms, weighted by TF-IDF
	- The query is encoded the same way
	- Score = cosine similarity between query vector and doc vector

> [!MATH] Cosine similarity
>
> $$\text{score}(q, d) = \frac{\vec{q} \cdot \vec{d}}{\lVert\vec{q}\rVert \, \lVert\vec{d}\rVert}$$

- Strengths
	- <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">Cheap, transparent, no training needed</mark> — works the moment you have an [[202605011617 - inverted index|inverted index]]
	- Strong baseline for keyword-style queries
- Weaknesses
	- <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">No semantic understanding</mark> — "car" and "automobile" are unrelated terms
	- <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Linear in tf</mark> — a document with 100 mentions of a query term scores ~100× higher than one with a single mention, even though the second mention contributes much less actual signal
	- Document length is handled crudely (just by L2 normalization)
- Successors
	- [[202605011627 - bm25|BM25]] addresses both weaknesses above with term-frequency saturation and length normalization
	- Dense retrievers ([[embeddings|embedding]]-based) address the semantic weakness directly
